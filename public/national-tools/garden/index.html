Why “last frost date” is not a deadline

Freeze climatology describes historical risk, not a guarantee that cold weather is finished. The frost tool keeps historical probabilities separate from the current NWS forecast so a warm spring or late cold snap does not disappear inside an average.

Why hardiness zone is a different question

USDA Plant Hardiness Zone is about perennial winter survival. It is useful context, but it is not a local last-frost date and should not be used by itself to schedule tomatoes, beans or lettuce.

Crop biology changes the planting answer

Cool-season crops can often tolerate conditions that would damage warm-season crops. The planting tool applies crop-specific rules to the local freeze anchor instead of giving every vegetable the same date.

Watering is not a fixed inch-per-week rule

Recent rain, evapotranspiration, rooting depth, crop stage, soil water-holding capacity, raised beds, containers, mulch and your own irrigation all change the answer. Garden Water keeps those inputs separate and shows uncertainty instead of pretending weather alone measures soil moisture.
