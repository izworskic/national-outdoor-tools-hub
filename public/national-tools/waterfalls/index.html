      How it works

      Two evidence paths, one useful answer

      A large waterfall may have a good network-connected streamgage and decades of same-date history. A smaller waterfall may have no gauge at all. Those should not be treated the same way.

      Gauged waterfallsUse hydrologically connected USGS observations, same-date percentile history, the local National Water Model reach and NWS precipitation context.
      Ungauged waterfallsUse the local modeled reach plus nearby active USGS streams normalized against their own seasonal history. The regional gauges describe watershed wetness; their CFS is never assigned to the falls.
      Why the narrative mattersThe page explains which path was used, what the signals agree on, what may change next and where uncertainty remains.
      Safety stays separateMore water can improve spectacle while increasing hazard. This tool does not determine trail status, closures, safe crossings, swimming conditions or safe viewing distance.

      National Water Model output is guidance rather than an official NWS river forecast. Regional proxy mode is intentionally capped below High confidence because no instrument is measuring discharge at the waterfall itself.
