Waterfall spectacle is a derived hydrologic decision

No single national feed says whether a waterfall is running unusually well today. Waterfall Window first links the waterfall to its hydrologic reach, then reads connected observations, local modeled flow, seasonal history and incoming precipitation together. If the evidence cannot support a comparative score, the score is withheld.

Flow and stage are different measurements

Discharge describes the volume of water moving past the gauge, while stage is water-surface height at that gauge. One should not be substituted for the other.

Trend often matters more than the snapshot

A river at the same flow can mean different things if it is rising quickly, falling after a crest or holding steady. The live river and waterfall tools use change as context rather than only the latest number.

Historical context answers “unusual for now?”

Same-season historical comparisons can show whether current flow is relatively high or low for the time of year. They do not make a recreational safety determination.

Official flood forecasts stay official

When a gauge can be matched to NOAA’s National Water Prediction Service, the river tool shows that forecast separately. National Water Model reach guidance in Waterfall Window is also labeled as model guidance rather than an official forecast.

Coastal water is a different decision

Beach risk, surf, buoy observations and tides do not belong inside an inland river or waterfall interpretation. The coastal tool keeps those sources separate and hands Michigan beach users to the deeper Great Lakes beach network.

Snowmelt is upstream context, not a river forecast

Measured snowpack and warm-weather melt pressure can explain one watershed input, but they do not prove when or how much a river will rise. Use the Snowpack tool for the pack itself and the river or waterfall specialist for the receiving water.

Safety boundary: A high waterfall spectacle score can coincide with more hazardous river edges, crossings and access. Gauge readings, model guidance and waterfall scores are environmental intelligence, not a paddling, swimming, wading, boating or safe-access determination.
