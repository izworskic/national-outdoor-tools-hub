How to use it

What would make this morning interesting?

The strongest use case is not a single score. It is the combination of an independently strong migration signal and workable local field conditions.

Strong migration + good weather

Worth checking productive local habitat. Radar tells you birds moved through the region; habitat still decides where they concentrate after dawn.

Strong migration + poor weather

The overnight movement may still be noteworthy, but rain or strong wind can make field birding difficult. In some situations weather can also concentrate migrants locally, which this tool does not attempt to predict.

Lower migration signal

Do not treat that as “no birds.” Resident birds and smaller movements remain possible, and BirdCast's radar product is not a complete inventory of species on the ground.

Source boundaries

No invented birding percentage.

  • BirdCast: Cornell Lab of Ornithology radar-based nocturnal migration estimates supply regional movement evidence such as migration amount, direction, speed and altitude when available.
  • Season: BirdCast's live feed operates during spring migration from March 1–June 15 and fall migration from August 1–November 15. Outside those windows the tool reports the feed as off-season rather than fabricating a live result.
  • Weather: National Weather Service hourly forecasts provide local morning rain and wind context. Our “good / mixed / poor” field-weather label is a simple transparent rule, not a BirdCast rating.
  • Geography: The tool attempts county-level BirdCast geography using U.S. Census boundaries, with state-level fallback when needed.
  • Species: Radar cannot identify the exact species in flight. Use BirdCast's species-migration resources and local observations for species expectations.

Bird migration data source: BirdCast Migration Dashboard, Cornell Lab of Ornithology. Weather source: National Weather Service.
